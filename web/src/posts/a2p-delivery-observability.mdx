Bulk SMS pricing can look simple until a campaign reaches real volume.

A rate that appears small per message can become a major monthly expense when your business sends OTPs, payment alerts, delivery updates, reminders, and promotions to thousands of customers.

To budget accurately, you need to understand more than the advertised unit price. Message length, destination, sender requirements, delivery attempts, and monthly commitments can all affect the final cost.

This guide explains how Ghanaian businesses should evaluate SMS pricing before selecting a provider.

## How SMS pricing is usually calculated

Most providers charge for outbound SMS based on the destination and the number of message segments sent.

The basic formula is:

> Number of billable segments × price per segment = estimated messaging cost

This means 10,000 customer notifications do not always equal 10,000 billable SMS segments. A long message may be split into two or more segments.

For accurate forecasting, estimate both message count and average segments per message.

## Message length can increase the cost

SMS messages have character limits. When a message exceeds the available characters for one segment, it is divided into several connected segments.

The exact limit depends on the characters used. Certain symbols, emojis, and non-standard characters can change the encoding and reduce the available characters per segment.

Consider these two campaign styles:

Short version:

> Save 20% at Akwaaba Home this weekend. Show code HOME20 at checkout. Terms apply.

Long version:

> Akwaaba Home is excited to announce our special weekend promotion where customers can receive 20% off selected home and office furniture. Visit our showroom and present promotional code HOME20 at checkout. Terms and conditions apply.

The longer version may require several segments and can multiply the campaign cost.

Keep SMS copy concise and move detailed terms to a trusted web page when appropriate.

## Destination matters

SMS rates vary by country because delivery involves different networks, commercial agreements, sender rules, and routing conditions.

A company serving Ghana and Kenya should not assume both destinations have the same cost. Under Dugble's current pricing, Growth is $0.0120 for Ghana and $0.0180 for Kenya.

When forecasting regional expansion, estimate traffic country by country. A blended average can hide expensive routes and make product margins difficult to understand.

## Failed messages and retries can affect your budget

A failed message may still create processing costs depending on the provider and failure stage. Automated retries can also create additional sends.

Your application should distinguish between failures that may succeed after retry and failures that require customer data to be corrected.

For example:

- A temporary network issue may justify a controlled retry.
- An invalid phone number should not be retried repeatedly.
- An expired OTP should not be delivered as though it is still useful.
- A rejected sender identity should be fixed before more traffic is sent.

Blind retries increase cost without improving the customer experience.

## Delivery quality matters alongside price

The cheapest message is not valuable if it does not help the customer complete the intended action.

Evaluate providers using:

- Delivery reporting
- Message logs
- Webhook events
- Sender onboarding
- Support responsiveness
- Destination coverage
- API reliability
- Billing clarity

Run tests across the networks your customers actually use. Measure not only whether the API accepted the request, but whether customers received the message in time and completed the intended action.

## How to reduce your SMS costs

Businesses can control spend by following a few practical rules:

1. Keep messages within one segment when possible.
2. Clean phone numbers before sending.
3. Remove duplicate contacts.
4. Send only to relevant and permitted audiences.
5. Stop retries after a final failure.
6. Cancel reminders when the customer has already acted.
7. Select a committed tier only when usage supports it.
8. Use email for detailed, non-urgent communication.
9. Monitor cost by destination and campaign.
10. Review delivery performance regularly.
