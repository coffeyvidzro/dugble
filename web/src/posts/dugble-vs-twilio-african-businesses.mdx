## 1. SMS pricing can reshape your product economics

SMS costs are easy to ignore when an application is small. The difference becomes significant when a product sends 10,000, 100,000, or one million OTPs and customer alerts each month.

As of July 31, 2026, Twilio lists outbound SMS to Ghana at $0.3741 per segment. Twilio notes that prices may change and that additional carrier fees can apply. You can review the current rate on the [official Twilio Ghana pricing page](https://www.twilio.com/en-us/sms/pricing/gh).

Dugble uses a committed-use and volume-based discount model:

| Dugble plan | Monthly commitment | Ghana SMS | Kenya SMS | Email rate |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Growth | None | $0.0120 | $0.0180 | $0.00050 |
| Scale | $100 | $0.0110 | $0.0165 | $0.00030 |
| Enterprise | $300 | $0.0100 | $0.0150 | $0.00020 |

Every plan includes 1,000 free emails each month.

At the Growth rate, 10,000 single-segment SMS messages to Ghana cost $120. At Twilio's listed Ghana rate, the same message volume has a base cost of $3,741 before any additional applicable fees.

That difference can fund engineering, customer support, infrastructure, or growth campaigns. For a startup, communication pricing is not only an infrastructure concern. It can directly affect runway and margins.

## 2. Start without a commitment, then reduce rates as you grow

Dugble does not require an early-stage business to predict its future volume.

The Growth plan has no monthly commitment. A team can launch, measure real traffic, and move to Scale or Enterprise when the lower rates justify a predictable monthly spend.

The Scale plan provides lower SMS and email rates with a $100 monthly commitment. Enterprise reduces those rates further with a $300 monthly commitment.

This model gives growing businesses a clear path:

1. Start with pay-as-you-go pricing.
2. Measure actual SMS and email usage.
3. Commit only when the savings make financial sense.
4. Receive better unit economics as communication volume grows.

The monthly commitment should be understood as committed communication usage, not a separate fee for accessing the platform.

## 3. Billing and payment operations matter

Many African businesses earn revenue in local currency while paying international software providers in US dollars.

That can introduce foreign exchange exposure, international card restrictions, payment failures, and additional finance work. A reliable messaging service is less useful when topping up the account becomes a recurring operational problem.

Dugble is designed around regional payment workflows. The goal is to let businesses fund communication services through supported local payment methods while developers continue using a consistent API.

Available currencies and payment methods should always be confirmed inside the Dugble dashboard before funding an account.

## 4. One workspace for SMS and transactional email

Most customer journeys use more than one communication channel.

A fintech product may send an OTP by SMS and a transaction receipt by email. An ecommerce business may send an order confirmation by email and a delivery alert by SMS. A marketplace may use email for routine updates and SMS for urgent security notifications.

Managing separate providers can mean separate credentials, invoices, webhook formats, message logs, and support processes.

Dugble brings SMS and transactional email into one workspace. Teams can monitor usage by channel, inspect delivery activity, and manage customer communication without switching between unrelated platforms.

## 5. Regional context can improve the operating experience

Successful SMS delivery depends on more than a valid API request. Sender identity, destination networks, message length, encoding, traffic classification, and local requirements can all affect delivery.

Twilio's strength is broad global coverage. Dugble's opportunity is depth in the African markets its customers use most.

That means helping businesses understand sender setup, providing useful delivery information, and offering support that recognizes local network conditions.

The value is not a promise that every message will always arrive instantly. The value is a platform that helps your team understand what happened and what to do next.

## When Twilio may be the better choice

Twilio may be appropriate when most of your users are outside Africa, your company requires products that Dugble does not yet offer, or your organization already operates a large Twilio implementation.

Twilio also has a mature international ecosystem, extensive documentation, and a long operating history. Those are meaningful advantages for some companies.

## When Dugble may be the better choice

Dugble may be a stronger fit when:

- Most of your customers are in African markets.
- SMS cost has a significant effect on your margins.
- Your application sends OTPs, transaction alerts, or operational notifications.
- You want SMS and transactional email in one workspace.
- You value regionally relevant billing and support.
- You want transparent rates that improve with committed usage.
