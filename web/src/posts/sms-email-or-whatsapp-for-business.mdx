Your business has an important message to send. The next question is where to send it.

Should it be an SMS, an email, or a WhatsApp message?

The correct answer depends on urgency, message length, customer consent, cost, and the action you want the customer to take. Choosing the wrong channel can lead to missed alerts, unnecessary costs, and frustrated customers.

A strong communication strategy does not force every message through one channel. It gives each message the channel that best matches its purpose.

## Quick comparison

| Factor | SMS | Email | WhatsApp |
|---|---|---|---|
| Internet required | No | Yes | Yes |
| Best for | Urgent and concise alerts | Detailed and permanent communication | Interactive conversations |
| Typical message length | Short | Long | Medium to long |
| Rich media | Limited | Strong | Strong |
| Customer reply | Depends on sender type | Yes | Yes |
| Common use cases | OTPs, alerts, reminders | Receipts, statements, newsletters | Support, updates, conversations |
| Setup considerations | Sender rules and destination pricing | Domain reputation and deliverability | Templates, consent, and platform policies |

## When SMS is the best choice

SMS works well when the message is urgent, short, and important even when the customer has no mobile data connection.

Common SMS use cases include:

- One-time passwords
- Transaction alerts
- Delivery updates
- Appointment reminders
- Service interruption notices
- Security warnings
- Time-sensitive payment reminders

SMS is especially useful when your customer base includes people using different types of phones or inconsistent internet connections.

The main limitation is space. SMS messages should be focused and direct. Long content may be divided into several billable segments, and special characters can affect message length.

A good SMS should tell the recipient who is sending it, what happened, and what they should do next.

## When email is the best choice

Email is better when the message needs detail, attachments, formatting, or a permanent record.

Common email use cases include:

- Welcome messages
- Receipts and invoices
- Monthly account statements
- Password reset links
- Product updates
- Newsletters
- Policy changes
- Detailed onboarding instructions

Email also gives businesses more room for branding, structured content, and several links.

The trade-off is urgency. Customers may not check their inbox immediately, and a message can land in spam or a promotions folder if sender reputation and authentication are not managed properly.

Transactional email should come from an authenticated domain with SPF, DKIM, and DMARC configured correctly. Marketing email should also include clear consent and unsubscribe controls.

## When WhatsApp is the best choice

WhatsApp is useful for interactive communication where the customer may need to reply, ask a question, or receive rich media.

Common use cases include:

- Customer support
- Order conversations
- Booking confirmations
- Product catalogues
- Delivery coordination
- Interactive service updates

WhatsApp can create a familiar and conversational experience, but businesses must account for template requirements, user consent, platform policies, and internet access.

It should not automatically replace SMS. A WhatsApp message may be missed when a customer has no data connection, has disabled notifications, or does not use the number for WhatsApp.

## Choose the channel based on the job

### OTP and login verification

Use SMS when broad reach and urgency matter. Email can serve as an alternative when the customer's email address is already verified.

Avoid sending the same OTP through several channels at once unless your security model is designed for it. Multiple codes can confuse customers and increase fraud risk.

### Transaction receipts

Use email for the full receipt because it supports detailed line items and a permanent record. Use SMS only when a short confirmation is valuable.

For example:

> Payment of GHS 250.00 was successful. Reference: DG48291. Your receipt has been sent by email.

### Delivery updates

SMS works well for short milestone alerts such as dispatched, arriving today, or delivered. WhatsApp may be useful when the customer needs to coordinate with a driver or support team.

### Marketing campaigns

Email is usually better for detailed promotions, images, and several products. SMS works well for a focused offer with a clear deadline. WhatsApp can support interactive campaigns when customers have provided appropriate consent.

Do not send the same promotion through every channel at the same time. That can make a campaign feel intrusive rather than useful.

### Account statements and reports

Use email. The content is detailed, often sensitive, and likely to be referenced later. SMS can notify the customer that the statement is available.

## Build a channel hierarchy

A communication hierarchy defines which channel should be tried first and what should happen if delivery fails.

For an urgent security alert, the hierarchy might be:

1. Send an SMS.
2. Track the delivery status.
3. Send an email if the SMS reaches a final failed state.
4. Show the alert inside the application.

For an order receipt, it might be:

1. Send the full receipt by email.
2. Send a short SMS confirmation.
3. Do not retry the SMS if the email has already delivered and the alert is not urgent.

The policy should be based on business importance, not on a desire to use every available channel.

## Prevent duplicate and noisy communication

Multichannel communication can easily become repetitive.

To keep it useful:

- Give each event a unique identifier.
- Store which channels have already been attempted.
- Define final and retryable delivery states.
- Add cooldown periods before fallback.
- Stop later sends when the customer completes the intended action.
- Respect channel preferences and consent.

For example, when a customer completes a payment after receiving an in-app prompt, a scheduled payment reminder should be cancelled before it reaches SMS or email.

## Consider cost as part of the decision

The cheapest channel is not always the best channel. A low-cost message that the customer never sees has little value. An expensive SMS for a non-urgent monthly newsletter may also be unnecessary.

Use SMS for messages where immediacy and reach justify the cost. Use email when detail and record keeping matter. Use WhatsApp when interaction provides clear customer value.
